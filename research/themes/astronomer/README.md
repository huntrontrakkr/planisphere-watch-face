# The Astronomer: instruments and observatory references

Status: in progress (sections being filled in one at a time)

Reference set for the "astronomer" card theme: a faint 60 x 25 px night scene
of Tycho Brahe's observatory and 20-24 px corner pieces of period instruments.
Public domain / CC0 only.

Notes on sources:

- **Rejected on licence:** the David Rumsey Map Collection copies of the Blaeu
  Uraniborg plates (IA `dr_arcis-vranibvrgi-103831377` and siblings, also
  mirrored on Commons as "(IA dr ...)" files and tagged PD there) carry the
  collection's own CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 terms in the IA rights field. They were
  downloaded and then deleted; none of the files here comes from them.
- Other copies of the same plates, if a file needs replacing: Royal Library
  Copenhagen 1598 copies on IA `den-kbd-pil-130018165806-001` and
  `den-kbd-pil-130018154081-001` (no rights field, but a 1598 book); Getty 1602
  copy IA `gri_tychonisbrah00brah` (NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT); Gallica `btv1b2600056p`
  (Commons "Illustrations de Astronomiae Instauratae Mechanica ... (NN of 28)",
  1024 px, PD); SLUB Dresden Fotothek scans of the 1598 (S.B.14) and 1602
  (Astron.82,misc.1) copies.
- **Not used, licence doubt:** Apian's *Instrument Buch* (Ingolstadt 1533),
  Wellcome copy on IA `hin-wel-all-00002040-001`. Every page carries the
  footer "Early European Books, Copyright (c) 2011 ProQuest LLC. Images
  reproduced by courtesy of The Wellcome Trust", and the item has no rights
  field, so it was not cropped. Its quadrant (n12-n15) is the same design as
  the 1532 *Quadrans Apiani* used here; its Jacob's staff figures (n110-n112)
  are covered by `../sea/images/apian1533-crossstaff.jpg` and
  `koebel1616-crossstaff.jpg`.
- No woodcut of a fully **assembled astrolabe front** (rete over plate, with
  throne) turned up in the PD sources searched (Stöffler 1513 and 1524 show the
  parts separately). Build the corner piece from the parts: the mater ring
  (`stoeffler1524-astrolabe-back-calendar`), the throne and ring
  (`stoeffler1524-astrolabe-throne-ring`), and the in-use figures; or use the
  mariner's astrolabe in `../sea/images/` (Eden 1561, Medina 1545).
- The 1598 and 1602 plates are the same blocks and copper plates; the 1602
  Smithsonian scans are uncoloured and crisp (about 3000 x 4900 px per page),
  the 1598 Copenhagen copy is hand-coloured and useful for the night-scene
  palette (blue-grey slate roofs, red-brown brick, green copper).
