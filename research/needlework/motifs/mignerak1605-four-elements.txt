notes: Mignerak's Four Elements are the only rain, lightning and cloud imagery found in this search of the period's needlework. Checked without result: contact sheets of every plate of Sibmacher 1604 and Vinciolo 1588 and of DMC Albums I and III; the c.1596 album's planet and flower pages (which have rayed suns but no clouds or rain); catalogue searches of the Met (sampler objects tagged Clouds, Rainbows, Lightning, Umbrellas: none), the V&A (keyword search; the one sampler viewed with clouds, T.168-1978 of 1746, works them in satin stitch, not counted) and the Rijksmuseum's 65 merklappen (windmills, no weather). What each square shows, as a guide for redrawing:
  EAU (Water): at the top, two clouds, each a row of three or four round puffs filled with the fine (darned) mesh and edged with a scalloped line; from the underside of each falls a fan of eight to ten long straight rain strokes, slanting slightly outward. This is the period's rain: scalloped cloud, straight streaks. Below them a nymph with streaming hair pours water from an urn among reeds.
  FEU (Fire): top left of centre, a sun with a face and wavy, flame-like rays; top right, a scalloped cloud with a burst of jagged, forked strokes darting from its left edge (fire from the cloud: lightning); flames rise from a brazier below; the figure is wreathed in flames.
  AIR: across the top, a band of billowing clouds, round puffs in a row with darned centres, over a chequered band (the sky); birds fly below and a figure floats among the clouds.
  TERRE (Earth): a crowned figure with fruiting branches, animals and plants; no weather.
  For the face at 7-15 cells: a cloud is a row of two or three round puffs on a flat base; rain is three to five straight slanting strokes under it; lightning is a forked zigzag leaving the cloud's edge; fog or wind can be a row of puffs drawn open (outline only), as Mignerak draws his clouds' edges.
