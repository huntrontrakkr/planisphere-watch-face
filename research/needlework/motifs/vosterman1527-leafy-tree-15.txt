title: Leafy tree (heart-shaped crown of six leaves on a trunk with a stepped foot)
section: Village
source: Willem Vosterman, Ce est ung tractat de la noble art de leguille, Antwerp, after 1527; The Met 53.566.3(1-46), Public Domain
short-source: Vosterman c.1527 (Met DP355625)
page: image DP355625, fourth band from the top (16 rows: this tree, a lily plant in a pot, a bird, a lattice plant, a bird); the tree is the first motif, x801-1161 y1384-1791 of the full scan
url: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/358594
image: https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/dp/original/DP355625.jpg
size: 15 x 15 cells
symmetry: mirror left-right
colours: # = black cell of the woodcut (worked), . = white cell
use: village (a tree; also a tree of life on a mound) / season (summer tree in leaf)
status: VERIFIED
verified: two reads on the white-cell lattice: (1) white-cell blob reader and (2) darkness at each site (Otsu): both 223/225. The 2 differences are isolated cells outside the tree (row 7 col 15 and row 12 col 5) where the worn block has filled a white cell with ink. A first read had one more cell above the crown (centre-left of the top row); both readers call it white on this crop, so it is left out. Cross-check: the same band cut separately in Zoppino's Esemplario di lavori, Venice 1529 (Met DP364477, plate XI, sixth band, x1419-1681 y1463-1721) has the identical tree, 225/225 cells (darkness read; blob read 224/225), on a much cleaner print: images/vosterman1527-leafy-tree-15-zoppino_ver.jpg. Looked at both overlays: images/vosterman1527-leafy-tree-15_ver.jpg
notes: Six leaves (stepped diamonds outlined in black, three each side) meet a 5-cell bar on the trunk; a pair of 2-cell branches and a second bar lower down, then a bare trunk and a 3-and-5 stepped foot. The page is heavily inked (white cells shrink to small blobs), so the overlay is dark. A good 1x tree: dense crown, clear trunk. Zoppino's 1529 copy of the band (crop images/vosterman1527-leafy-tree-15-zoppino.jpg) is the better reference image. The same tree stands in Quentel's Cologne band of 1529 (quentel1529-tree-15: identical except for a 6-cell bottom row), so this band of trees, lilies, birds and lattice hearts was shared by the Cologne, Antwerp and Venice books.
