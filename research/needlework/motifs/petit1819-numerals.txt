size: 7 rows; widths 1:3, 2:4, 3:5, 4:5, 5:6, 6:5, 7:7, 8:4, 9:5, 0:7
symmetry: 1, 8 and 0 mirror left-right and top-bottom; the others none
colours: # = stitch (printed blue-grey on a fine grid of 2x2 squares per stitch), . = ground
use: numerals (ring hours, the date): a printed period set, all figures on one baseline
status: VERIFIED
verified: the page scan is small (one stitch = 6.7 px), so each figure was read on its own lattice: stitch pitch = 2 squares of the printed grid, phase chosen per figure as the one that makes the cells cleanest (86-99 % of cells clearly empty or clearly full); every figure was also checked by eye at 9x zoom with the lattice drawn. Overlays of the whole line on two lattices (1-6, and 7-0 which the engraver set half a stitch lower): 292/294 and 210/210 cells agree (images/petit1819-numerals_ver.jpg). The two disagreements: ink of the 1's top serif spreading into the cell below, and the corner of the 6's bowl, where the stroke runs diagonally across a cell boundary (read here as empty, as the cleanest lattice for the 6 shows).
notes: The line is closed at both ends by a small hollow diamond (.#. / #.# / .#.). The 1 is the same forked-serif I as the 1596 album's (album1596-numerals); the 5 keeps the old hooked flag at the top; the 7 is a long diagonal under a 5-cell bar; the 0 is a wide oval. Compare the same plate's snowflake star and capitals.
