title: Three-branched tree (tree of life sprig on a stepped base)
section: Village
source: Peter Quentel, Eyn New Kunstlichboich, Cologne 1529; The Met 20.50.2(1-49), Public Domain
short-source: Quentel 1529 (Met DP361383)
page: image DP361383, band 5 of 7, first motif at the left; x560-790 y1245-1480 of the full scan (band crop: images/quentel1529-band5.jpg)
url: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/354657
image: https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/dp/original/DP361383.jpg
size: 15 x 15 cells
symmetry: mirror left-right about the trunk (column 8), except the base row, which the woodcut cuts 6 cells wide, one cell off centre
colours: # = black cell of the woodcut, . = white cell
use: village (a tree) / season (the leaves are chevrons: spring or summer tree)
status: VERIFIED
verified: darkness of each lattice site (sites from the white-cell lattice), Otsu threshold, compared with the chart: 222/225 agree; the 3 others are cells of the neighbouring filler crosses outside the tree. Overlay: images/quentel1529-tree-15_ver.jpg
notes: Three leaf clusters (two outward chevrons and a central diamond on a stem) on stems that gather into a trunk, standing on a stepped base. For a symmetric base use 5 cells (.....#####.....) or 7. The same tree, cell for cell, is in Willem Vosterman's pattern book (Antwerp, after 1527; see vosterman1527-leafy-tree-15) with exactly that symmetric 5-cell base: the early pattern books copied one another, so this tree is a shared period form.
