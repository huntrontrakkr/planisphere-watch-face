# Labours of the Months: woodcut references for the tools

Reference set for drawing the tools of each month's labour as 20-24 px pixel-art
vignettes. Every image here is public domain. The notes describe what is
**actually drawn** in each cut: the object, the shape of its head, the length of
the haft against the figure, and how it is held.

All files are in `images/`, named `<source>-<MM>-<month>.<ext>`. Where a page
held several scenes, the file is cropped to the month's labour.

Notes on sources:

- The English *Kalender of Shepherdes* printers (Pynson 1506, de Worde, 1556)
  used only the **January** block of the French set. The 1556 January page
  (`shepherdes1556-01-january.jpg`) is the same block as `kalendrier1529-01`,
  so it is not an independent source for January. Its October page shows a
  separate small vignette: a man treading grapes in a vat.
- Not used: Wellcome's images of Sommer's 1892 facsimile (CC BY 4.0, not
  public domain); British Museum images (CC BY-NC-SA); the Met's Kerver
  *Horae* of 1504 (CC0, accession 20.53.3), because only four leaves are
  photographed and none is a calendar page; EEBO microfilm scans on archive.org
  (licence unclear).
- `vostre1496` follows the Paris Hours calendar, which runs a month later than
  the Kalendrier for some labours: Vostre puts **sowing in September** and the
  **vintage in October**, where the Kalendrier has the vintage in September
  and sowing in October. In both French series **June is sheep-shearing and
  July is mowing**. In the German calendar July is mowing.
- `kalendrier1529-00-wheel.jpg` and `shepherdes1556-00-wheel.jpg` are the same
  wheel of the months. Its segments are too small and crowded to read reliably,
  so it is kept for interest only.

originals, saved at 2x) and the Belot vignettes (about 260 px originals, saved
at 2x) only show overall shapes.

## Suggested silhouettes for 20-24 px

Rule of thumb from the cuts: the tools that read are **one long straight
line with a single distinctive head**: scythe, flail, peel, pole, rake, spade,
axe. Scenes that depend on a figure's posture or on texture (fire, foliage,
sheaves, grapes) do not.

| Month | Best (1-3) | Avoid (too fussy at 20 px) |
|---|---|---|
| Jan | **Lidded flagon** (pear body, domed lid, loop handle: reads at 10-12 px tall); flagon + beaker pair; or crossed **knife** over a round plate | Roast fowl on a plate; the fireplace hood |
| Feb | **Pothook with a hanging cauldron** (a vertical chain plus a round pot); a **bundle of logs** tied with a band; small flames under the pot to set the scene | Bellows (no source shows them clearly); a figure warming his foot |
| Mar | **Pruning hook / serpette** (short handle, beak-hooked blade); **spade** (long shaft, square shod blade) | Vine stems and props; tying bundles |
| Apr | **Posy on a stem** (round flower ball on a stick); **garland ring** | The falcon-and-heron fight; feathered hat |
| May | **Falcon on a gloved fist** (bird silhouette on a forearm stub); **May bough** (a long stick with a leafy crown) | The horse (too big and detailed); the couple riding |
| Jun | **Sheep shears** (two straight blades with a round bow at the end: unmistakable); **shepherd's crook** | The fleece basket; a sheep carried on the shoulders |
| Jul | **Scythe** (a long straight snath with a hook-like crescent blade at one end; draw it upright as in V, or carried blade-up as in K); add the **whetstone horn** as a small tapered shape; the **hay rake** is a good second | The peening anvil and hammer; haystacks; the costrel |
| Aug | **Toothed sickle** (semicircle on a short handle; 2-3 notch pixels on the inner edge are enough); **sheaf** tied at the waist (hourglass bundle) | Scattered stalks; the reaping posture |
| Sep | **Hooped vat / tub** with grape dots; **hotte** (tall cone-shaped back-basket); **grape pail** with a bail handle | Barrels lying on their side (read as logs); vine stakes |
| Oct | **Flail** (long handstaff with a short club hinged at the tip); **harrow** (a square grid with tooth-pixels on one side); **seed sack** | The wheeled plough with its team; scattered seed |
| Nov | **Long acorn pole** with a few acorns falling; **axe** (broad squared head); a single **acorn** as a motif | The pig and trough (a body shape, not a tool); the blood pan |
| Dec | **Bread peel** with 2-3 round loaves on the blade (long haft plus a square end: very legible); **axe in a chopping block** (a stump with a slanted haft) | Domed ovens (a flat lump at small size); the hanging balance |

Shapes worth keeping exactly as the woodcutters drew them:

- **Scythe:** the snath is nearly straight and longer than a man; the blade is
  a thin crescent, not a fat hook. In V it rises vertically and arcs over the
  head, which makes a clean tall glyph. In K the second scythe is carried over
  the shoulder, blade up. The side grip is a short peg about a third of the way
  down the snath.
- **Flail:** the swingle is short and stubby (about 1/3 of the handstaff) and
  hangs at an angle from the tip. Draw it as two segments with a one-pixel gap
  or kink at the joint.
- **Sickle (August):** a semicircle with the teeth on the inner edge; the
  handle is short and in line with one end of the arc.
- **Shears (June):** two parallel straight blades with an O-shaped spring bow
  at the grip end; the blades are about twice the length of the bow.
- **Bread peel:** a long thin haft and a flat square or rectangular blade,
  carrying round loaves drawn as dots or circles.
- **Pole (November):** about twice the man's height, thin, and angled up into
  the tree.
- **Harrow:** a rectangle of crossing bars with teeth on one long side.
