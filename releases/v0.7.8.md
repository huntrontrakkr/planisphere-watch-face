The constellations shown when asked, the sky spun a whole day round, and two flicks to ask.

A flick that shows the constellations now works at any hour and on every plate except the Astrolabe, whose rete carries no figures. The window shows the sky as a printed chart would: the night sky and its brighter stars, each constellation's figure drawn in, star to star, in solid lines in the plate's own colours, with the constellations' names over them. The other names are left off while it shows. It holds a moment, then the face comes back.

A flick that spins the sky now turns it a whole day round. It starts gently, through dusk, the night with its stars and names, and dawn, and settles back on the hour, in about two seconds.

A new setting, How many flicks, asks for one flick or two. With two, the second must come within four seconds of the first: two quick flicks, or one to light the screen and one more. Two is the default, so a flick by chance sets nothing off, and settings from before keep the flick you chose but now ask for two.

The settings page plays the constellations and the spin as the watch does.

A frame of the face also needs less memory: the sky's grid is let go before the time is drawn, and the bright stars' two tables are now one.
